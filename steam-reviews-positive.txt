Albeit the Warlock of Firetop Mountain is the original digital book form as opposed to the 3D turn-based RPG that was separately Kickstarted. For those not aware or don't remember, Fighting Fantasy is a long-running series of various books where you, the reader, are the hero/heroine of the story. The book speaks to you in second person as both the narration and characters. You create a character by your imagination and then roll dice to determine your Skill, Stamina and Luck and all three will be used at some point(s) in the story. You take damage, you deal damage, you can die and you may witness other characters dying, some as a direct result of your own actions, but mostly because you will "learn" the gamebook's ideal path the more you play.
                  - text: For newcomers, this is right up your alley as you can "pick up and play" easily as there is a detailed but optional ruleset as well as the history of Fighting Fantasy, so you can become fully aware of what you're getting into and how to play these gamebooks. Not only that but as I just mentioned, these are just some of the finest Fighting Fantasy gamebooks out of dozens and dozens throughout the decades. I recommend playing the gamebooks in release of issue order, which can be arranged as such in the options cogwheel, this is so that you're playing them in the original row of releases the way the writers wanted their audience to experience them.
                  - text: For veterans, it's a near 1:1 conversion if your favourites put into digital form with many quality of life conveniences such as the bookmark system, which works self-explanatory, automated dice rolling and stat changes, gallery section and page turning at the click of a button. You can view both the classic and "new" artwork at the click of a button.
                  - text: Overall, this is a must-have for gamebook lovers, my favourite is Deathtrap Dungeon so I would recommend you'd give that a play through if you don't want to follow the issue release order. This is also the first time Deathtrap Dungeon has been made available digitally on PC.
                  - text: Finally, the interface of this collection itself is nice, the selection of audio tracks is a welcome addition and it's nice to see the books on a shelf so to say. My gripes are the lack of a "mouse hover" effect, no dedicated quit button and the resolution option changing is buggy as it takes a few clicks for the changes to register.
