# Interactive Fiction & Choice-Based Games: Comprehensive Research Analysis

> Research compiled for informing the design of a new gamebook platform.

---

## 1. 80 Days (Inkle Studios, 2014)

**Platforms:** iOS, Android, Windows, Mac, Nintendo Switch  
**Metacritic:** 88/100 (iOS), 84/100 (PC)  
**Word Count:** ~750,000 words (player sees ~2% per playthrough)

### Core Mechanics
- **Route-based exploration** — Players freely choose their path around a steampunk 1872 globe. Dozens of cities are visitable, each with unique narrative content. The 3D globe UI allows plotting travel routes between cities via trains, airships, submersibles, and other fantastical transport.
- **Resource management triad** — Three gamified resources: (a) Money (for transport, hotels, items), (b) Fogg's Health (suffers from strenuous journeys, limiting route options), (c) Time (the 80-day deadline). All three are woven into narrative decisions.
- **Market/item economy** — Buy/sell items in different markets around the globe. Items unlock narrative options (e.g., a gift can open doors with certain NPCs).
- **Massive branching** — Each complete circumnavigation reveals approximately 2% of total text. Unseen content unlocks on replay, incentivizing multiple playthroughs. Hidden endings, easter eggs, and references to Verne's other works.
- **Naturalistic choice presentation** — Choices flow from the narrative rather than feeling mechanical. Dialogue and action options emerge organically from story context.
- **Persistent character relationships** — Fogg evolves from aloof to human across the journey. NPCs are richly characterized and leaving them behind carries emotional weight.

### UI/UX Approach
- **Split-screen layout** — 3D globe + narrative text panel. Minimalist, elegant design.
- **Touch-native origins** — Designed for iOS first, ported later. UI remains clean and gesture-friendly on all platforms.
- **Map-centric navigation** — The globe serves as both world map and travel planning tool.
- **Stats always visible** — Money, health, and day counter persistently shown.

### Accessibility
- **Text-heavy but skimmable** — No voice acting; purely text-driven.
- **Color contrast** — Good visual contrast in UI elements.
- **No documented screen reader support**, but simple, clean text layout.
- **Multiple platforms** — Available on essentially all major platforms including Switch.

### What Makes It Special
- Won **IGF Excellence in Narrative**, **BAFTA nominations** (Best British Game, Best Story, Mobile Game, Game Innovation), and **Writers' Guild of Great Britain award**.
- The Telegraph named it "one of the best novels of 2014" — a game recognized as literature.
- **Steampunk reimagining** of Verne's classic adds sci-fi wonder (walking cities, mechanical gondoliers).
- **Themes of colonialism, racism, and cultural encounter** handled with surprising nuance.
- Extremely replayable — 7+ playthroughs still reveal new content.

### Strengths
- Industry-leading prose quality
- Seamless blend of game mechanics and narrative
- Replayability without feeling repetitive
- Handles mature themes intelligently

### Weaknesses
- No save system (auto-save only; commitment to choices)
- Time pressure can frustrate completionist players
- Limited character customization (you are always Passepartout)

## 2. Choice of Games / Hosted Games (2009–Present)

**Platforms:** Web, iOS, Android, Kindle, PC/Mac/Linux  
**200+ titles** spanning 15+ years  
**Word counts:** 30,000 to 1,376,197 words per game

### Core Mechanics
- **ChoiceScript engine** — Custom proprietary scripting language designed specifically for multiple-choice interactive fiction with a "small number of variables." Openly available for community authors.
- **Stat-based branching** — Core innovation: numeric stats (e.g., Strength 65%, Charm 40%) that gates choices. If your stat is high enough, an option is available; if not, it's grayed out (or leads to failure). This is the defining CoG mechanic.
- **Opposed-pair stats** — Many games use opposed stat pairs (e.g., Ruthless vs. Merciful, Cunning vs. Honorable) creating meaningful trade-offs.
- **Character customization** — Gender, sexuality, name, background are commonly customizable. Known for diverse portrayals of gender and sexuality.
- **Save/load + checkpoint system** — Players can save, reload, and restart from checkpoints. Lower-stakes than auto-save-only games.
- **Achievement system** — In-app achievements for reaching specific endings or stat thresholds.
- **Three-tier publishing model** — (a) Choice of Games (in-house developed/edited), (b) Hosted Games (community-submitted, revenue-shared), (c) Heart's Choice (romance-focused label, launched 2019). This model has produced massive volume.

### UI/UX Approach
- **Pure text + stats sidebar** — No illustrations, no map, no animations. Text in center, stats column on side.
- **Percentile stats display** — Stats shown as numeric percentages and/or bars.
- **Grayed-out unavailable choices** — Shows what you *can't* do (with hover/click explaining why), which makes stats feel tangible.
- **Minimalist aesthetic** — Black text on light background. Extremely lightweight, loads instantly.
- **Chapter structure** — Games are divided into chapters with title cards.

### Accessibility
- **Screen reader compatibility** — Explicitly noted as accessible to the visually impaired. Pure text format is inherently screen-reader-friendly.
- **Font size options** — Adjustable text size in mobile apps.
- **Dyslexia-friendly font option** — Available in some implementations.
- **Keyboard-only navigation** — No mouse required.
- **No timed choices** — Players can take as long as they want.

### What Makes It Special
- **Democratized IF creation** — ChoiceScript and the Hosted Games model lets anyone publish. This has produced breakout hits like *The Wayhaven Chronicles*, *Zombie Exodus: Safe Haven*, *Tin Star* (1.37 million words).
- **Stats as narrative** — The stat system makes character-building feel consequential. "I have 70% Persuasion so I can talk my way out of this" creates a tangible RPG feel in pure text.
- **Long-running ecosystem** — 15+ years of continuous operation with a dedicated fan community.
- **Serialized sagas** — Multi-book series (*Heroes Rise*, *Samurai of Hyuga*, *The Lost Heir*) build loyal readership.

### Strengths
- Proven, scalable business model (community + curated)
- Excellent accessibility
- Deep stat-based RPG systems in pure text
- Massive content library

### Weaknesses
- Stat gates can feel arbitrary or gamey ("Sorry, you need 71% Strength and you have 70%")
- No visual/multimedia elements (by design, but limits audience)
- Variable writing quality (especially Hosted Games)
- UI feels dated and utilitarian
- Minimal sense of place or spatial navigation

## 3. Disco Elysium (ZA/UM, 2019)

**Platforms:** Windows, macOS, PS4/PS5, Switch, Xbox, Stadia, Android  
**Metacritic:** 91/100  
**Sales:** 5+ million copies  
**Awards:** The Game Awards 2019 (Best Independent Game, Best Narrative, Best RPG, Fresh Indie Game); BAFTA for Best Debut

### Core Mechanics
- **The 24-skill system** — Four primary attributes (Intellect, Psyche, Physique, Motorics) each with 6 sub-skills (24 total). Skills are NOT just passive modifiers — they are active voices in the protagonist's head. They interject in dialogue, argue with each other, and with the player. A high Drama skill might detect lies but also make you prone to hysterics.
- **Skill checks as conversation** — Dice-roll-based checks (2d6 + skill + modifiers). Failing checks is often as interesting as succeeding. The game is designed around "embracing failure."
- **The Thought Cabinet** — An internalization system. Players "equip" thoughts that take in-game hours to process, then grant permanent bonuses (and sometimes penalties). Example: "Hobocop" thought — while internalizing, NPCs react negatively; once complete, doubles money from recycling trash. Can "forget" thoughts for a skill point cost.
- **Dialogue combat** — No traditional combat. All conflict is resolved through skill checks and dialogue trees. Internal debates with your own skills are a core mechanic.
- **Dialogue as gameplay** — 1.2+ million words, fully voice-acted in The Final Cut. The narrator and 24 skills alone represent ~half the dialogue.
- **Clothing as stats** — Equipped clothing imparts both positive and negative effects on skills.
- **No traditional fail state** — You can't really "lose." The game continues through failures, and failure often produces the best writing.

### UI/UX Approach
- **Isometric painterly visuals** — Stunning oil-painting aesthetic by Aleksander Rostov.
- **Dialogue in right panel** — Conversation choices in orange, narrative in white, displayed in a scrolling panel on the right side of the screen.
- **Skill portraits** — Each of the 24 skills has a unique illustrated portrait and personality.
- **Thought Cabinet UI** — Grid-based interface for managing internalized thoughts.
- **Full voice acting** (*The Final Cut*) — 100+ characters fully voiced. Narration and skill voices by jazz musician Lenval Brown. Voice acting is optional and toggleable.
- **Soundtrack by Sea Power (British Sea Power)** — Original compositions + reworked existing tracks. Critically praised.

### Accessibility
- **Full voice acting** (*The Final Cut*) — Makes text-heavy game accessible to those who struggle with reading large volumes.
- **Voice acting toggle** — Can choose narrator-only, characters-only, or all voice.
- **Text size options** — Available in settings.
- **Colorblind options** — Some visual accessibility features.
- **No time pressure** — No real-time elements; players can take as long as needed.
- **Controller support** — Full gamepad support on all console versions.

### What Makes It Special
- **Revolutionary approach to skills** — Skills as characters/fragments of the protagonist's psyche. This turns the internal monologue into a dialogue. "Inland Empire" (a Psyche sub-skill) is described as "the intensity of the soul" and may help you pass yourself off under a fake identity — not because it makes you better at lying, but because it makes you believe your own lie.
- **Marxist-materialist worldbuilding** — 6,000+ years of in-universe history. The world was constructed with attention to Marx's theory of historical materialism. The political dimension is not cosmetic — it's structural.
- **"Microreactivity" design philosophy** — Instead of trying to cover all possible choices, the game focuses on small acts and decisions cascading through the world. The skill system then provides internal commentary on these cascading effects.
- **Embracing failure as a design principle** — Explicitly designed around the experience of failure, inspired by Kurvitz's own depression and alcoholism after his novel commercially failed.
- **No combat in a CRPG** — Proved that a traditional RPG framework can work with zero combat.

### Strengths
- Unparalleled writing quality and depth
- Skills-as-characters is a genuinely novel mechanic
- Political and philosophical depth
- Full voice acting in Final Cut

### Weaknesses
- Limited replayability for some (long game, slow pacing)
- Some players find the political content heavy-handed
- Console ports had performance issues at launch
- Thought Cabinet can feel opaque (internalization takes real time)
- No mobile version until 2025 (Android visual novel port)

## 4. The Life and Suffering of Sir Brante (Sever / 101XP, 2021)

**Platforms:** Windows, PS4, Xbox One  
**Metacritic:** Positive (specific score varies)

### Core Mechanics
- **Lifespan-spanning narrative** — The game follows Sir Brante from birth to death through life stages: childhood, adolescence, college, professional career, and the "Revolt." Each chapter represents a different era in the character's life.
- **Choose Your Own Adventure-style branching** — Classic gamebook format with stat-based gates and branching paths based on accumulated choices.
- **Relationship and stat system** — Player choices contribute to statistic changes and relationship values that gate future options and determine which endings are accessible.
- **Class/social hierarchy mechanics** — Born to parents of different social ranks (commoner mother, noble father), creating inherent tension. The game's social hierarchy is a core mechanic and theme.
- **"Lesser Deaths" system** — In the game's theology, commoners get 3 "Lesser Deaths" (resurrection with a black mark) before their 4th "True Death" triggers final judgment. This creates a lives system baked into the narrative fabric.
- **Multiple radically different endings** — Ranging from the Empire surviving with reforms, to the gods abandoning humanity in disgust, to Sir Brante achieving godhood through heresy.

### UI/UX Approach
- **Text-centric with atmospheric illustration** — Dark fantasy aesthetic. Illustrated backgrounds and character portraits accompany text.
- **Stat display** — Visible character sheet showing accumulated traits, relationships, and social standing.
- **Book-like chapter structure** — The lifespan framing creates a novel-like reading experience.
- **Choice presentation** — Options shown as text cards, sometimes with stat requirements visible.
- **Moody, gothic aesthetic** — Dark color palette, parchment-like textures, period-appropriate typography.

### Accessibility
- **Text-heavy** — Pure text-driven, suitable for screen readers (though not explicitly documented).
- **No time pressure** — Turn-based/pause-anytime.
- **No voice acting** — Text only.
- **Limited accessibility features documented** — Smaller studio, fewer resources for accessibility.

### What Makes It Special
- **Lifespan as game mechanic** — The entire life arc, from birth to death, is the game. This is rare even in IF. You witness the consequences of childhood decisions playing out decades later.
- **Theological worldbuilding** — The Twins (Elder and Younger gods), the Pillar of the universe, the Lesser Deaths system — the theology is deeply integrated into gameplay.
- **Social class as core system** — Not just a theme; the game's mechanics revolve around navigating and potentially subverting the rigid social hierarchy.
- **Russian literary sensibility** — Developed by a Russian studio (Sever, based in Tomsk), the game carries the weight and fatalism of Russian literary tradition.

### Strengths
- Lifespan-spanning narrative creates long-term consequence
- Deeply integrated theology and social class mechanics
- Dark fantasy aesthetic well-executed
- Multiple genuinely different endings

### Weaknesses
- Reported software bugs that can ruin endings (RPGFan review)
- Linear chapter structure reduces feeling of agency between chapters
- Limited replayability (long game, mostly same early-game content)
- No German translation at launch (for German market)
- UI is somewhat utilitarian

## 5. Roadwarden (Moral Anxiety Studio, 2022)

**Platforms:** Windows, macOS, Linux, Nintendo Switch  
**Metacritic:** 85/100  
**Engine:** Ren'Py

### Core Mechanics
- **40-day time limit** — On standard difficulty, the player has 40 in-game days to complete their mission before autumn forces return. Travel, resting, and certain actions consume time. Optional easy mode removes the timer.
- **Open-world exploration** — The peninsula is explored freely via a sectioned layout: illustration pane (left) showing map with fog of war, text/dialogue pane (center), and stats/actions pane (right).
- **Character class system** — Choose between Fighter, Mage, or Scholar, each with unique knowledge and dialogue options. Character background (religion, etc.) also affects NPC interactions.
- **Survival elements** — Six tracked resources: Vitality, Nourishment, Appearance, Armor, Pneuma (mana), and Currency ("dragon bones"). All must be maintained to survive and succeed.
- **Free-text input prompts** — The game occasionally asks players to type word prompts (e.g., what to say to a specific character). Creates mystery and discovery.
- **Dice-roll choices** — Random chance-based decisions marked with a die icon. Unlike many games, odds are NOT displayed — only prose describes the outcome. This preserves narrative immersion.
- **Quest journal** — Tracks discovered quests and developments, but deliberately does not hold the player's hand. Players are encouraged to keep their own physical notebook.
- **No "good" ending** — Whatever choices are made, there are winners and losers. The game resists clean resolutions.

### UI/UX Approach
- **Three-panel layout** — Left: pixel art illustration/map with fog of war. Center: text and choices with side-scrolling. Right: stats panel (date, time until dusk, vitality, nourishment, armor, appearance, pneuma).
- **Pixel art aesthetic** — Each area has a unique pixel art illustration. Evocative and atmospheric.
- **Ambient music** — Changes with mood and location.
- **Physical notebook encouraged** — The game is designed for players to take real-world notes, enhancing the explorer fantasy.
- **Clickable dialogue choices** — Standard visual novel style choice presentation with some free-text input prompts.

### Accessibility
- **Ren'Py engine** — Built on Ren'Py, which has some built-in accessibility features (self-voicing mode, text size).
- **No voice acting** — Text only.
- **No time pressure in easy mode** — Timer can be turned off.
- **No quick-time events** — All interactions are text-based and pausable.
- **Limited documented accessibility** — Small studio, fewer resources.

### What Makes It Special
- **The notebook as a mechanic** — The game is explicitly designed around the player keeping external notes. This is not a flaw — it's a feature that deepens the "explorer making field notes" fantasy.
- **Unknowability as design** — The peninsula can't be fully understood in one playthrough. You're a stranger for ~40 days. You can't fix everything, can't make everyone happy, can't know everything. This is intentional and thematic.
- **Sensory writing** — Critically praised for invoking taste, smell, texture. The writing makes you *feel* the rough wool blanket, the hearty stew, the cold forest floor.
- **Complex ecological worldbuilding** — The uneasy coexistence of humans and nature on the peninsula is central. The impact of human actions on the land is described in detail.
- **Built on Ren'Py** — Proves that a visual novel engine can host deep RPG mechanics.

### Strengths
- Superb atmospheric writing
- Exploration and mystery feel genuine
- Survival mechanics add tension without being punishing
- Free-text input creates real moments of discovery
- Pixel art is evocative

### Weaknesses
- Some decisions not reflected in ending (RPGFan criticism)
- Free-text input can be frustrating if exact wording is unclear
- Time limit may stress some players
- Limited visual variety over long play sessions
- Relatively small word count compared to CoG/80 Days

## 6. Twine (twinery.org, 2009–Present)

**Creator:** Chris Klimas  
**License:** GPL v3 (open source)  
**Current Version:** 2.10.0  
**Written in:** TypeScript (v2), Python (v1)  
**Notable works:** *Depression Quest*, *Howling Dogs*, *Queers in Love at the End of the World*, *The Uncle Who Works for Nintendo*, *Black Mirror: Bandersnatch* (used for scripting)

### Core Mechanics
- **Visual hypertext editor** — Node-based visual editor where each "passage" is a box and links are arrows between boxes. No programming knowledge required.
- **Story formats** — Rather than a fixed scripting language, Twine supports multiple "story formats" (dialects): Harlowe (default, beginner-friendly), SugarCube (powerful, based on Twine 1), Snowman (JavaScript integration), Chapbook (by Klimas, "second generation").
- **Variables and conditionals** — All story formats support variables, if/else logic, and state tracking.
- **CSS styling** — Full CSS customization for appearance.
- **JavaScript extensibility** — SugarCube and Snowman allow JavaScript for complex logic.
- **Export as HTML** — Stories compile to a single self-contained HTML file that runs in any browser.
- **Proofing formats** — Special formats for error checking, proofreading, and format conversion.

### UI/UX Approach
- **Browser-based + desktop app** — Available as a web app and standalone desktop application.
- **Node graph editor** — Passages displayed as draggable boxes connected by arrows. Very visual, intuitive for non-programmers.
- **Dual-pane editing** — Edit passages in one pane, see the story map in another.
- **Preview mode** — Test stories directly in the editor.

### Accessibility
- **HTML output** — Stories are web pages, inheriting web accessibility features (screen readers, zoom, custom CSS).
- **Keyboard navigable** — Output is standard HTML with keyboard-navigable links.
- **No built-in accessibility tools** — Relies on browser/platform accessibility. Story accessibility depends on author implementation.
- **Low barrier to entry** — The tool itself is accessible to non-programmers, which is a different kind of accessibility.

### What Makes It Special
- **Democratized interactive fiction** — Twine made IF creation accessible to people who had never coded. This spawned an entire movement of indie IF, including works about mental health (*Depression Quest*), queer experience (*Queers in Love at the End of the World*), and experimental narrative.
- **Used by Charlie Brooker** — *Black Mirror: Bandersnatch* (Netflix's interactive film) was scripted/planned using Twine.
- **Community and culture** — Massive community of creators, extensive documentation, easily shareable (single HTML files).
- **Educational tool** — Widely used in schools and universities for teaching narrative design.
- **The "Twine revolution"** — Coined by games critic Cara Ellison in The Guardian. Transformed who could make and publish games.

### Strengths
- Free and open source
- Zero programming knowledge required for basics
- Highly extensible for advanced users (JavaScript, CSS)
- Massive community and documentation
- Single-file HTML output is trivially shareable
- Available on all desktop platforms

### Weaknesses
- No built-in save/load system (must be implemented per story format or custom-coded)
- Variable/scoping can be confusing for beginners across story formats
- Performance degrades with very large stories
- No built-in multimedia asset management (images/audio need manual HTML)
- No native mobile export (runs in mobile browser, but not as an app)
- Each story format has different syntax/limitations — fragmentation

## Synthesis: Lessons for Building a New Gamebook Platform

### 1. Stats Should Serve Narrative, Not Gate It Arbitrarily

| Good | Bad |
|------|-----|
| Disco Elysium: Skills *are* characters that speak to you. Failing a check is as interesting as passing. | CoG: "You need 71% Strength, you have 70%." Feels arbitrary and gamey. |
| 80 Days: Money/health/time create organic constraints that feel like real travel problems. | Stat gates that simply block content with no alternative path. |

**Lesson:** Design stats that enrich the experience even when they "fail." Consider skills as personality traits that open AND close options, or that change the *tone* of success rather than binary pass/fail.

### 2. The Best UI is Often Split-Screen

All five games use some form of partitioned layout:
- **80 Days:** Globe + narrative
- **Roadwarden:** Illustration + text + stats
- **Disco Elysium:** Scene + dialogue panel
- **CoG:** Stats sidebar + text
- **Sir Brante:** Illustration + text

**Lesson:** A three-panel or two-panel layout is the proven pattern. Consider: [Illustration/Map] | [Narrative + Choices] | [Stats/Inventory]. The illustration panel provides atmosphere and spatial grounding; the stats panel provides game-state visibility.

### 3. Time Limits Are Divisive — Offer Choice

- **80 Days:** 80-day limit creates urgency but many players fail first playthrough (which is by design).
- **Roadwarden:** 40-day limit; easy mode removes it entirely.
- **Disco Elysium:** No time limit (Thought Cabinet internalization takes in-game hours, but you can wait indefinitely).
- **Sir Brante:** Lifespan chapters are sequential, not timed.

**Lesson:** If you include a time limit, offer an "explorer mode" that removes or extends it (like Roadwarden). Time pressure can enhance theme but shouldn't gate accessibility.

### 4. Voice Acting is Transformative — When Optional

Disco Elysium: The Final Cut added 1.2 million words of voice acting, making it accessible to dyslexic players and those who prefer audio. But it's toggleable (narrator only, characters only, all, or none).

**Lesson:** If resources allow, partial voice acting (key moments, character barks) with full voice as a stretch goal. Always make it optional.

### 5. Platform Strategy: Web First, Native Second

| Platform | 80 Days | CoG | Disco Elysium | Sir Brante | Roadwarden | Twine |
|----------|---------|-----|---------------|------------|------------|-------|
| Web | No | Yes | No | No | No | Yes |
| iOS | Yes | Yes | No | No | No | Browser |
| Android | Yes | Yes | Yes (2025) | No | No | Browser |
| Windows | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes | App |
| Console | Switch | No | PS/Xbox/Switch | PS/Xbox | Switch | No |

**Lesson:** Web + mobile is the broadest reach for IF. A gamebook platform should target: (a) responsive web app, (b) mobile apps (iOS/Android), (c) desktop apps, (d) console as stretch.

### 6. Authoring Tools Are a Platform Multiplier

Both Inkle (inklewriter → Ink/Inky) and Choice of Games (ChoiceScript) built authoring tools that:
- Created community and ecosystem
- Generated free content through user submissions
- Provided a pipeline for discovering talent
- Made their platform the default for a generation of IF authors

Twine proved this at *massive* scale — open-source, no-code, community-driven.

**Lesson:** A new gamebook platform should include an authoring tool, even a simple one. The tool IS the platform's moat. Consider: a web-based visual editor (Twine-like) that outputs to your platform's format, with variable/condition support.

### 7. Accessibility Must Be Designed In, Not Bolted On

CoG's pure-text format is inherently screen-reader friendly. Twine outputs HTML which inherits web accessibility. Roadwarden's easy mode removes time pressure. Disco Elysium's fully-voiced Final Cut opened the game to new audiences.

**Must-haves for a new platform:**
- **Screen reader compatibility** (semantic HTML, ARIA labels)
- **Adjustable text size** (at minimum; ideally full typography controls)
- **Font options** (including dyslexia-friendly fonts)
- **No time-limited choices** (or optional timer toggle)
- **Keyboard-only navigation**
- **High contrast mode**
- **Self-voicing option** (TTS integration for text passages)
- **Save anywhere** (not just checkpoints)

### 8. Content Volume and Replayability

- **80 Days:** 750K words, 2% seen per playthrough → extreme replayability through content volume.
- **CoG:** 30K–1.3M words per game. Stat variations + branching = multiple distinct playthroughs.
- **Disco Elysium:** 1.2M words. Less replayable (long game, same core plot), but Thought Cabinet + skill builds create variation.
- **Roadwarden:** Completing everything is impossible in one playthrough by design → replayability through mystery.
- **Sir Brante:** Multiple endings, but replaying early life stages is repetitive.

**Lesson:** Replayability can come from (a) sheer content volume (80 Days), (b) mechanical variety (Disco Elysium skill builds, CoG stats), or (c) designed incompleteness (Roadwarden). The most satisfying approach is probably (a) + (b): enough content that no single playthrough sees everything, AND mechanical variety that makes each run feel different.

### 9. Thematic Depth Over Power Fantasy

All five narrative games handle serious themes:
- **80 Days:** Colonialism, cultural encounter, class
- **Disco Elysium:** Addiction, depression, political ideology, failure, late capitalism
- **Sir Brante:** Social class, religious heresy, fatalism
- **Roadwarden:** Ecological relationships, the limits of individual action
- **CoG:** Diverse gender/sexuality representation, political rebellion (*Choice of Rebels*)

None are simple power fantasies. The best IF treats the player as an adult.

**Lesson:** A gamebook platform should support and encourage thematic depth. The tooling shouldn't limit authors to simple "hero's journey" templates.

### 10. Engine Choice Matters

| Game | Engine | Notes |
|------|--------|-------|
| 80 Days | Inkle's Ink + Unity | Custom narrative engine powering Unity game |
| CoG | ChoiceScript | Proprietary IF-specific language |
| Disco Elysium | Unity | Standard game engine |
| Sir Brante | Unknown (likely custom) | — |
| Roadwarden | Ren'Py | Visual novel engine |
| Twine | Browser (HTML/JS/CSS) | Self-contained web app |

**Lesson:** For a new platform, consider: (a) Web-native (HTML/JS/CSS) for broadest reach, (b) custom narrative engine for authoring ease, (c) export targets for mobile/desktop. Ren'Py is a strong existing option if building a game rather than a platform. Ink (open source, MIT license) is excellent for narrative scripting within a larger game.

### 11. What None of These Games Do Well (Opportunity Gaps)

- **Multiplayer/co-op IF:** None offer shared narrative experiences. A gamebook platform could support "party mode" where choices are voted on, or co-authoring where two players inhabit different characters.
- **Cross-platform save sync:** Only CoG (via account) does this. Cloud saves across web/mobile/desktop would be a differentiator.
- **Multimedia integration:** Most are text-only or text + static illustration. Room for ambient audio, subtle animation, dynamic illustration that changes based on stats/mood.
- **Accessibility-first design:** None were designed from the ground up for accessibility; all added features later.
- **Modding/community content marketplace:** CoG has Hosted Games. Twine has community. But no platform has a Steam Workshop-like content marketplace for IF.
- **Live/GaaS elements:** None have live events, daily stories, or seasonal content. A platform could offer serialized "episodes" released weekly.

### 12. Recommended Feature Set for a New Gamebook Platform

Based on all research, a new gamebook platform should include:

**Core Engine**
- Branching narrative with variable/condition support
- Stat system that enriches rather than gates (Disco Elysium model preferred)
- Save/load/checkpoint system (save anywhere)
- Choice history/log (players can review past decisions)

**Authoring Tool**
- Visual node-based editor (Twine-like)
- Variable and condition editor (no-code where possible)
- Preview/test mode
- Export to platform format

**UI/UX**
- Three-panel layout: [Illustration/Map] | [Narrative] | [Stats/Inventory]
- Dark/light mode
- Typography controls (font family, size, line spacing)
- Responsive (works on mobile, tablet, desktop)

**Accessibility**
- Full screen reader support (semantic HTML, ARIA)
- Dyslexia-friendly font option
- High contrast mode
- Self-voicing / TTS integration
- Keyboard-only navigation
- No time pressure (or toggleable)

**Platform**
- Web app (primary)
- iOS + Android apps (wrapped web or native)
- Desktop apps (Electron or similar)
- Cloud save sync
- Offline support (PWA)

**Community**
- Author marketplace / sharing
- Ratings and reviews
- Content discovery (tags, genres, word count, play time)
- Achievement system
